    <p>The Kumar Research Group is delighted to congratulate <strong>Nathan Carey</strong> on delivering an excellent third-year PhD talk today, titled:</p>

    <blockquote class="showcase-quote">
        <p><strong>From Rust to Remedy: Harnessing Siderophore-Antimicrobial Peptide Mimic Conjugates to Combat Antimicrobial Resistance</strong></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Nathan's research tackles one of the most pressing challenges in modern medicine: bacteria that no longer respond to existing antibiotics. Antimicrobial peptides and their mimics can directly disrupt bacterial membranes, giving them a mode of action that bacteria find difficult to resist. Nathan's work links these membrane-active compounds to <strong>siderophores</strong>, the small molecules bacteria produce to scavenge iron (the "rust" in his title) from their surroundings. Because bacteria actively take up siderophores to meet their iron needs, these conjugates are designed to be carried into the cell by the bacteria's own uptake systems.</p>

    <figure class="post-figure">
        <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/nathan-third-year-talk/talk2.jpg" alt="Nathan Carey explaining how antimicrobial peptides and their mimics disrupt bacterial membranes" class="featured-image">
        <figcaption>Nathan explaining how antimicrobial peptides and their mimics can directly disrupt bacterial membranes.</figcaption>
        <p class="photo-credit">Photo credit: Dr. Maryam</p>
    </figure>

  
    <p>The third-year talk is an important milestone in the PhD journey, and Nathan's presentation showed both the depth of his research and how far he has come. Congratulations, Nathan! The Kumar Research Group is proud of your progress and looks forward to seeing this work continue to contribute to the fight against antimicrobial resistance.</p>
