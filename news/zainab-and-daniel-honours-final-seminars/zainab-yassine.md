    <p>Congratulations to <strong>Zainab Yassine</strong>. The Honours final seminar made a hard biological idea feel urgent, then showed the chemistry built in answer to that idea. The seminar was titled:</p>

    <blockquote class="showcase-quote">
        <p><strong>Synthesis and biological evaluation of dual-acting quorum sensing inhibitors against <em>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</em></strong></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>The title is the project. Zainab Yassine makes the inhibitors, then tests the effect on <em>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</em>. Quorum sensing is the way bacteria take a census. Once the crowd is large enough, virulence, biofilm behaviour, and the traits that make an infection so difficult to clear switch on. <em>P. aeruginosa</em> is especially fluent in that language: a critical priority pathogen, naturally resistant to many of the drugs clinicians reach for first, and well practised at coordinating an attack.</p>

    <p>A dual-acting inhibitor is designed to do two jobs in one molecule: silence that bacterial conversation, and hit the cell by a second mechanism. Resistance then has to solve two problems at once. Zainab Yassine laid that case out from the opening slides on antibiotic resistance, through the biology of <em>P. aeruginosa</em>, to the conclusions and where the synthesis and biological evaluation go next.</p>
