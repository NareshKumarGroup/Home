    <p>Congratulations to <strong>Sam</strong> and the team on the publication of a new paper in <em>Antibiotics</em>!</p>

    <p><strong>Background/Objectives:</strong> There is a growing demand for the development of novel antimicrobial agents due to their efficacy being eroded by increasing antimicrobial resistance. Antimicrobial hydrogels have been reported as a method to treat bacterial infections.</p>

    <p><strong>Methods:</strong> This study explores how different structural features are important for the hydrogelation properties of amphiphilic antimicrobial peptide-mimics through rheology and AFM, as well as properties important for antimicrobial activity measured through MIC.</p>

    <p><strong>Results:</strong> Eleven novel peptide-mimicking anthranilamides containing various structural features were synthesised in 4–7 steps. Of these peptide-mimics, three novel compounds formed hydrogels, and it was identified that their mechanical strength, secondary structure, and fibre morphology could be tuned by altering the aromatic cap or the amino acid side chain. In conjunction, several structural features were identified that reduce hydrogelation strength and stiffness.</p>

    <p><strong>Conclusions:</strong> This work provides an insight into how the structural features of low-molecular-weight self-assembling hydrogels can translate to differing physical and potent antimicrobial properties. This work provides a rational guide to optimising physical hydrogel properties, as well as highlighting features that may reduce hydrogelation.</p>

    <p><strong>Publication details:</strong></p>
    <ul>
        <li><strong>Journal:</strong> Antibiotics</li>
        <li><strong>Title:</strong> Structural Features of Antimicrobial Peptide-Mimic Hydrogels</li>
        <li><strong>DOI:</strong> <a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/antibiotics14111118" target="_blank">10.3390/antibiotics14111118</a></li>
        <li><strong>Authors:</strong> Muel T. Attard, Vina R. Aldilla, Rajesh Kuppusamy, Renxun Chen, David StC. Black, Pall Thordarson, Mark D. P. Willcox, and Naresh Kumar</li>
        <li><strong>Published:</strong> November 5, 2025</li>
    </ul>

    <p>Congratulations to Sam and all co-authors on this outstanding achievement! This work provides valuable insights into the design of antimicrobial hydrogels and their applications in treating bacterial infections.</p>
