    <p>We're thrilled to congratulate <strong>Ghayah Bahatheg</strong> and all co-authors on the publication of a new paper in <em>RSC Advances</em>!</p>

    <p>What if we could keep everything that makes antimicrobial peptides powerful, and lose what holds them back?</p>

    <p>That's the promise of peptoids. They mimic how natural antimicrobial peptides attack bacteria, but their modified backbone makes them much harder for enzymes to break down.</p>

    <p>The article, <strong>Recent progress in the structure-activity relationship of antibacterial peptoids</strong>, surveys recent advances in that design space, bringing together structure-activity insights that will help guide the next generation of antibacterial peptoid scaffolds.</p>

    <p><strong>Publication details:</strong></p>
    <ul>
        <li><strong>Journal:</strong> RSC Advances (2026)</li>
        <li><strong>Title:</strong> Recent progress in the structure-activity relationship of antibacterial peptoids</li>
        <li><strong>Authors:</strong> Ghayah Bahatheg, Tope Abraham Ibisanmi, David StC. Black, Mark D. P. Willcox, and Naresh Kumar</li>
        <li><strong>DOI:</strong> <a href="https://doi.org/10.1039/d6ra07372g" target="_blank" rel="noopener">10.1039/d6ra07372g</a></li>
    </ul>

    <p>Congratulations to Ghayah and all co-authors on this outstanding achievement! The Kumar Research Group is proud of this contribution to antibacterial peptoid research.</p>
