    <p>We're thrilled to congratulate <strong>Samuel T. Attard</strong> and co-authors on the publication of a new paper in <em>RSC Advances</em>!</p>

    <p>The article, <strong>Synthesis of nitric oxide releasing conjugated <em>ortho</em>-hindered nitroarenes and their application in dual-action antimicrobial peptide-mimics</strong>, reports a new class of nitric oxide–releasing antimicrobial peptide-mimics. By combining membrane disruption with controlled nitric oxide release, these compounds offer a dual-action strategy for combating bacterial infections.</p>

    <p>Building from a previously established anthranilamide peptide-mimicking scaffold, the team rationally substituted aromatic capping groups for aromatic conjugated <em>ortho</em>-hindered nitroarenes (CONAs). The CONAs were accessed via a novel synthetic pathway that avoids highly toxic CrO<sub>3</sub> and red fuming nitric acid, delivering the targets in fewer steps and good-to-excellent yields compared with earlier methods. One of the peptide-mimics formed a self-assembling low-molecular-weight hydrogel, characterised by rheology, atomic force microscopy, and circular dichroism. The compounds released NO upon exposure to 530 nm light, and antimicrobial activity was assessed by MIC assay — with one compound active against Gram-positive <em>Staphylococcus aureus</em> at 8 µg mL<sup>−1</sup>.</p>

    <p><strong>Publication details:</strong></p>
    <ul>
        <li><strong>Journal:</strong> RSC Advances (2026)</li>
        <li><strong>Title:</strong> Synthesis of nitric oxide releasing conjugated <em>ortho</em>-hindered nitroarenes and their application in dual-action antimicrobial peptide-mimics</li>
        <li><strong>Authors:</strong> Samuel T. Attard, Md Aquib, Hatu Gmedhin, Cyrille Boyer, Pall Thordarson, David StC Black, and Naresh Kumar</li>

    <p>Congratulations to Sam and all co-authors on this outstanding achievement! The Kumar Research Group is proud of this dual-action antimicrobial work and excited to see where the CONA platform leads next.</p>
