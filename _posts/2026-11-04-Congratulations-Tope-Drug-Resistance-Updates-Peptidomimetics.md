    <p>The Kumar Research Group is delighted to congratulate <strong>Tope A. Ibisanmi</strong> and all co-authors on a new review now available online in <em>Drug Resistance Updates</em>.</p>

    <p><strong>Advancements in Peptidomimetics as a Strategy Against Multidrug Resistance: Fundamentals, Rational Design, Resistance Mechanisms, and Artificial Intelligence</strong> brings together the chemistry, biology, and emerging computational approaches in this area. Antimicrobial resistance continues to challenge effective treatment, and new approaches to antibacterial discovery are urgently needed.</p>

    <p>The review discusses major peptidomimetic scaffold classes, how their properties shape antibacterial activity, how resistance mechanisms such as membrane remodelling may undermine them, and where molecular modelling and artificial intelligence could contribute. A central contribution is an integrated developability framework. It asks whether a peptidomimetic’s potency holds under physiological conditions, remains robust against bacterial adaptation, and can meet translational constraints. For each scaffold, the framework asks what mechanism it exploits, which resistance liability may threaten it, and what clinical indication its properties could realistically support.</p>
